# Why T₂? Universal Properties and the Algebraic Boundary of Attention

This document gives two interconnected results. Part 1 argues that the truncated tensor algebra T₂(V) is not an arbitrary architectural choice but the *algebraically forced* answer to a precise question. Part 2 characterizes what softmax attention gains by breaking the monoid structure, and asks whether the break is necessary.

---

## Part 1: Why T₂? The universal property argument

### The monoid constraint (review)

The score decomposition theorem (`Kleisli.agda`) forces any sequential predictor with fixed-size state `S` to satisfy a monoid homomorphism property. The state after `xs ++ ys` must be determinable from the states after `xs` and `ys` independently. This forces `(S, combine, s₀)` to be a monoid and the map `Char* -> S` to be a monoid homomorphism.

This is not a design choice. It is a consequence of the specification.

### The embedding step

Each character `c` in the alphabet Sigma maps to an embedding `e_c` in `V = R^d`. This is a function `Sigma -> V`. The embedding dimension `d` is a hyperparameter, typically much smaller than `|Sigma|` (we use d=2 to 16 vs. |Sigma|=27).

With embeddings in hand, the monoid constraint becomes: find a monoid `(S, *, s₀)` and a map `phi : V -> S` such that the accumulation `phi(e_{c_1}) * phi(e_{c_2}) * ... * phi(e_{c_n})` captures enough information about the sequence `c_1 c_2 ... c_n` to predict the next character.

### What is the "most informative" monoid over V?

This is the question that leads to T₂. We want a monoid `(S, *, s₀)` that:
1. Is **finite-dimensional** (we need fixed-size state for any practical model).
2. **Respects the vector space structure** of V (the embedding lives in a vector space, and we want to exploit its linear structure).
3. Is **universal** in the sense that it captures as much information as possible, given constraints (1) and (2).

Condition (2) means we want `S` to be an *R-algebra* (a vector space with an associative bilinear multiplication) and `phi` to be *R-linear* (or at least, the extension from single embeddings to sequences should be algebraic). This rules out arbitrary monoids and focuses on algebraic ones.

The question becomes: **what is the free associative R-algebra generated by V?**

### Answer: the tensor algebra

**Definition.** The *tensor algebra* `T(V)` over a vector space `V` is the direct sum:

```
T(V) = R + V + (V (x) V) + (V (x) V (x) V) + ...
```

with multiplication given by the tensor product (concatenation of tensors). An element of degree k is a k-tensor in `V^{(x)k}`. Multiplication of a degree-j element by a degree-k element gives a degree-(j+k) element.

**Universal property (Theorem).** `T(V)` is the *free* associative unital R-algebra on `V`. That is: for any associative unital R-algebra `A` and any linear map `f : V -> A`, there exists a unique algebra homomorphism `f_bar : T(V) -> A` extending `f`. In other words, `T(V)` is initial in the category of associative R-algebras equipped with a linear map from `V`.

*Proof sketch.* Define `f_bar` on degree-k tensors by `f_bar(v_1 (x) ... (x) v_k) = f(v_1) * ... * f(v_k)`, extended linearly. The algebra homomorphism property `f_bar(x * y) = f_bar(x) * f_bar(y)` follows from associativity of `A`. Uniqueness follows because any algebra homomorphism extending `f` must agree on generators, hence on all products of generators, hence on all of `T(V)`.

**Consequence.** If we want a monoid that is also an R-algebra and that "remembers everything" about the sequence of embeddings (up to the algebra structure), we must use `T(V)`. Any other algebraic monoid receiving `V` is a quotient of `T(V)`, hence loses information.

### Truncation: why finite order is necessary and why k=2

The full tensor algebra `T(V)` is infinite-dimensional -- the state would grow without bound as we process longer sequences (degree-k tensors accumulate for all k). We need to truncate.

**Definition.** The *truncated tensor algebra* `T_k(V)` is:

```
T_k(V) = R + V + (V (x) V) + ... + V^{(x)k}
```

with multiplication truncated: any product that would produce a term of degree > k is discarded (projected to zero).

`T_k(V)` has dimension `sum_{i=0}^{k} d^i`, where `d = dim(V)`.

| k | State space | Dimension (d=16) | What it captures |
|---|------------|------------------|-----------------|
| 0 | R | 1 | Character count only |
| 1 | R + V | 17 | Count + sum of embeddings (unigram statistics in embedding space) |
| 2 | R + V + (V (x) V) | 273 | Count + unigram + pairwise outer products (bigram statistics in embedding space) |
| 3 | R + V + (V (x) V) + (V (x) V (x) V) | 4,369 | Adds trigram correlations |

**Why k=2 is the sweet spot:**

*Statistical argument.* If the true text distribution is first-order Markov (each character depends on the previous one), then the bigram counts `{N(a,b) : a,b in Sigma}` are *sufficient statistics* for the transition matrix. The degree-2 tensor `sum_t e_{c_{t-1}} (x) e_{c_t}` computes exactly these bigram statistics, but in the d-dimensional embedding space rather than the |Sigma|-dimensional one-hot space. T₂ computes **soft bigram counts** -- the sufficient statistics of the simplest non-trivial language model, lifted through embeddings.

This is worth stating precisely:

**Proposition (T₂ computes embedded bigram statistics).** Let `c_1, ..., c_n` be a character sequence with embeddings `e_1, ..., e_n` in `R^d`. The T₂ state after processing this sequence is:

```
s = (n, sum_t e_t, sum_t e_{t-1} (x) e_t)
```

The degree-2 component `M = sum_t e_{t-1} (x) e_t` satisfies: for any vectors `u, v in R^d`,

```
u^T M v = sum_t (u . e_{t-1})(v . e_t)
```

This is the *soft bigram count* for the embedding-space "bigram" `(u, v)`: how often a character whose embedding correlates with `u` is followed by one whose embedding correlates with `v`. When `d = |Sigma|` and embeddings are one-hot, this reduces exactly to the standard bigram count matrix.

*Proof.* Direct computation: `u^T (sum_t e_{t-1} e_t^T) v = sum_t (u^T e_{t-1})(e_t^T v) = sum_t (u . e_{t-1})(v . e_t)`.

*Dimensional argument.* The readout from T₂ state to logits (in `R^{|Sigma|}`) requires a linear map of size `|Sigma| * dim(T_k)`. For k=2, d=16: this is `27 * 273 = 7,371` parameters. For k=3: `27 * 4,369 = 117,963`. The readout cost grows as `O(d^k)`, making k >= 3 impractical without further structure (such as low-rank factorization of the readout, which is exactly what multi-head attention provides for k=2).

*Universal property argument.* T₂(V) is the "best approximation" to T(V) that captures all correlations up to order 2. Any algebra homomorphism from T(V) to a target algebra factors through the projection T(V) -> T_k(V) when the target has nilpotent elements of order > k (i.e., when the target cannot distinguish (k+1)-grams from k-grams). For models whose expressive power is limited to pairwise interactions, T₂ captures everything that can be captured.

### Summary of Part 1

The chain of reasoning is:

1. **Spec forces monoid.** Score decomposition requires state to be a monoid homomorphism from `(Char*, ++)` to `(S, *, s₀)`. (Proven in `Kleisli.agda`.)

2. **Embeddings give vector space structure.** Characters map to `V = R^d`, so we want `S` to be an R-algebra (a monoid that respects the linear structure of embeddings).

3. **Universal property selects T(V).** The tensor algebra `T(V)` is the free associative R-algebra on `V` -- it is initial, hence maximally informative. (Standard algebra; see e.g. Bourbaki, *Algebra*, Ch. III.)

4. **Finite-dimensionality forces truncation.** Practical models need `dim(S) < infinity`, so we truncate to `T_k(V)`.

5. **k=2 is the information-theoretic sweet spot.** T₂ computes soft bigram counts (sufficient statistics for first-order Markov models in embedding space), at manageable dimension `1 + d + d^2`. Higher orders grow as `d^k` and require more parameters in the readout than they provide in expressiveness.

6. **Readout structure is forced.** Given T₂ state (a `d x d` matrix plus lower-order terms), any linear readout decomposes into multi-head attention by rank decomposition. (Shown in `ATTENTION-ALGEBRA.md`.)

T₂ is not one choice among many. It is the answer to: **"What is the most informative finite-dimensional algebraic monoid over an embedding space, truncated at pairwise correlations?"** The universal property of the tensor algebra makes this precise.

## Part 2: What does softmax add? The algebraic boundary

Levels 1 through 3 of our hierarchy -- bigram, T₂, and linear attention -- are all monoid homomorphisms from `(Char*, ++)` to some `(S, +, 0)`. They differ in what gets accumulated and how the readout works, but the compositional structure is identical. Level 4, softmax attention, breaks this structure. This section characterizes what the break buys and whether it is necessary.

### The normalization problem

Linear attention computes, for a query `q` at position `t`:

```
output_t = (sum_{i<=t} phi(k_i) v_i^T) q = S_t q
```

where `S_t = sum_{i<=t} phi(k_i) v_i^T` is the accumulated state matrix. This is a monoid homomorphism: `S_{xs++ys} = S_{xs} + S_{ys}` (with appropriate reindexing).

Softmax attention computes:

```
output_t = sum_{i<=t} [exp(q_t . k_i) / sum_{j<=t} exp(q_t . k_j)] v_i
```

The denominator `Z_t = sum_{j<=t} exp(q_t . k_j)` depends on ALL keys seen so far. If we split the sequence into `xs ++ ys`, the attention weights for tokens in `xs` change when `ys` is appended, because `Z` grows. The map from sequences to outputs is NOT a monoid homomorphism.

### What normalization buys

**1. Competition and bounded attention weights.** In softmax attention, the weights `alpha_i = exp(q . k_i) / Z` sum to 1. Tokens compete for attention: if one key aligns strongly with the query, others are suppressed. In linear attention, the "weights" `phi(k_i)^T q` are unbounded and do not sum to any fixed value. More tokens means a larger unnormalized sum, which is why linear attention models typically divide by the count (as we do in T₂).

The consequence is that softmax attention can implement *selective retrieval*: "attend to exactly the token where character X appeared, ignoring everything else." Linear attention can only approximate this with soft, non-competitive weighting.

**2. Sharpness (temperature scaling).** Softmax attention can be made arbitrarily peaked by scaling: `softmax(beta * Q K^T)` approaches a hard argmax as `beta -> infinity`. This lets the model do precise, nearly discrete lookup. Linear attention with positive feature maps (e.g., ELU+1) produces inherently "soft" attention patterns. The attention entropy has a lower bound determined by the feature map, not a learnable parameter.

This matters for tasks requiring precise positional retrieval ("copy the character from position 3"), but matters less for statistical tasks ("predict the next character based on bigram frequencies"), which is why T₂ performs well on the name generation task.

**3. Data-dependent routing.** Because softmax weights depend on the query-key interaction AND the full context (through the normalization), the model can dynamically route information based on what it has seen. With a fixed-size monoid state, the model must commit to a compression of history *before* seeing the query. The query can select *from* the compressed state but cannot change *what was stored*.

This is the deepest difference. In monoid models (Levels 1-3), the state `S_t` is computed by a fixed fold over the input, independent of any future query. In softmax attention, the effective "state" is the full key-value history `{(k_i, v_i)}_{i<=t}`, and the query determines how it is read. The state is not compressed until query time.

### Is there a weaker algebraic property softmax satisfies?

Softmax attention is not a monoid homomorphism, but it is not algebraically lawless either. Several weaker structures apply:

**Softmax attention is a scan.** Causal softmax attention can be computed left-to-right with a growing (not fixed-size) state `{(k_i, v_i)}_{i<=t}`. It is sequential and causal, but not a fixed-size monoid.

**Block-wise decomposition (FlashAttention).** Dao et al. (2022) showed that softmax attention can be computed block-by-block. For blocks `B_1, ..., B_m`, the state for each block is a pair `(numerator, denominator)`:

```
numerator_j   = sum_{i in B_j} exp(q . k_i) v_i
denominator_j = sum_{i in B_j} exp(q . k_i)
```

These combine across blocks by weighted addition:

```
numerator_{1..m}   = sum_j numerator_j
denominator_{1..m} = sum_j denominator_j
output = numerator_{1..m} / denominator_{1..m}
```

This is a monoid! The state `(numerator, denominator)` in `R^{d_v} x R` combines by component-wise addition, and the final output is the ratio. But there is a critical caveat: **the numerator and denominator depend on the query `q`**. For each different query, you get a different monoid. The state is not query-independent.

More precisely, define for a fixed query `q`:

```
S_q(xs) = (sum_{i in xs} exp(q . k_i) v_i,  sum_{i in xs} exp(q . k_i))
```

Then `S_q` IS a monoid homomorphism from `(Char*, ++)` to `(R^{d_v} x R, +, 0)`, and the output is `pi_1(S_q) / pi_2(S_q)` (ratio of first component to second).

**Proposition (Softmax attention as query-indexed monoid).** For each query `q`, the softmax attention numerator-denominator pair `S_q` is a monoid homomorphism. Softmax attention is a *family* of monoid homomorphisms indexed by the query, followed by a nonlinear (rational) readout.

*Proof.* `S_q(xs ++ ys) = S_q(xs) + S_q(ys)` because the sum over `xs ++ ys` splits into the sum over `xs` plus the sum over `ys`. The combination operation is component-wise addition in `R^{d_v} x R`. The zero element is `(0, 0)`. Associativity and identity follow from addition.

The monoid BREAKS because:
- The monoid itself (i.e., which homomorphism to use) depends on the query.
- The query is computed from the current token, which is known only at readout time.
- Therefore the state cannot be pre-accumulated independently of the query.

In monoid models (Levels 1-3), the state is accumulated ONCE and queried MANY times (once per vocabulary element for logits). In softmax attention, each query induces a DIFFERENT weighting of the history, so the "state" cannot be shared across queries.

### The Performers / random feature approximation

Choromanski et al. (2020) showed that softmax attention can be approximated by linear attention with a specific random feature map:

```
softmax(q . k) ~ phi(q)^T phi(k)
```

where `phi` maps vectors to a higher-dimensional feature space via random projections and exponential nonlinearities. With this approximation, the monoid property is restored: `S_t = sum_{i<=t} phi(k_i) v_i^T` is a fixed-size matrix, and the output is `phi(q_t)^T S_t` (possibly with a count-based normalizer).

The ELU+1 feature map we tested (replacing `exp` with `ELU(x) + 1 = max(0, x) + 1` for `x >= 0`, `exp(x)` for `x < 0`) is a deterministic variant of this idea. Empirically, it improved our multi-head ProjT₂ from NLL=2.258 (H=4) to NLL=2.250 (H=4 with ELU+1), matching T₂(R^16) flat readout at half the parameters.

The algebraic reading: softmax attention is *approximately* a monoid homomorphism, with the quality of the approximation depending on the feature map. The gap between linear and softmax attention is the gap between the best finite-dimensional feature map and the exact (infinite-dimensional) one.

### Synthesis: the monoid boundary

The four levels of the hierarchy divide into two regimes:

**Sufficient statistics models (Levels 1-3).** State is a monoid. History is compressed into a fixed-size summary before any query is evaluated. The monoid structure guarantees compositionality: the state for `xs ++ ys` is determined by the states for `xs` and `ys` independently. The best monoid for pairwise correlations in embedding space is T₂(V), by the universal property of the tensor algebra. Multi-head readout from T₂ state is forced by rank decomposition.

These models compute *sufficient statistics* in the information-theoretic sense. For a first-order Markov source, T₂ captures everything. For higher-order dependencies, T_k with k >= 3 is needed, but the dimension grows as `d^k`.

**Full attention models (Level 4).** State grows with the sequence (the full key-value cache). Queries induce data-dependent weightings over the full history. The monoid property holds only when conditioned on a specific query, and different queries see different "compressions" of the same history. This is strictly more expressive: the model can implement arbitrary retrieval patterns that depend on both the history and the current position.

The algebraic story:
- If you want **fixed-size state**, you MUST use a monoid homomorphism. The universal property of the tensor algebra tells you: T_k(V) is the best such monoid at order k, and T₂ is the sweet spot for pairwise correlations.
- If you want to capture **beyond-pairwise correlations** without growing the state as `d^k`, you MUST leave the monoid regime. Softmax attention is one way; its query-indexed decomposition shows it is a *family* of monoids (one per query) combined with a nonlinear readout.
- **Feature map approximations** (Performers, ELU+1) let you stay within the monoid regime at the cost of approximation quality. The gap between linear and softmax attention is precisely the gap between the best finite-dimensional feature map and the exact infinite-dimensional one.

## What is proven, what is conjectured, what is speculative

**Proven (in this project):**
- Score decomposition forces monoid structure (`Kleisli.agda`, formal Agda proof).
- T₂ state update is a monoid homomorphism by construction (`TensorBigram.agda`).
- Any linear readout from D x D matrix state decomposes into multi-head attention (`ATTENTION-ALGEBRA.md`, informal proof; formalizable).
- T₂(R^16) achieves NLL=2.210-2.256 on 32k names, beating Karpathy's MLP (empirical).

**Standard results invoked:** Universal property of T(V) (Bourbaki, *Algebra* III; Lang, *Algebra* XVI). Sufficient statistics for Markov chains (Cover & Thomas, Ch. 2). FlashAttention's block-wise decomposition (Dao et al., 2022).

**Conjectured:** T₂ is optimal among order-2 algebraic monoids for prediction (universal property gives information-completeness, but optimality also depends on readout and distribution). The k=2 sweet-spot argument is empirically supported but not formalized.

**Speculative:** Whether the algebraic hierarchy mirrors a statistical hierarchy (T_k = sufficient statistics for order-k Markov). Whether structured T₃ variants (symmetric tensors, tensor trains, Clifford algebras) can avoid the `d^3` blowup. Whether the query-indexed monoid property of softmax can be formalized as a "fibered monoid" with its own universal property.

## Open questions

1. **Optimality of T₂.** Can we prove that among all d^2-dimensional monoids over R^d, T₂ maximizes some information-theoretic criterion (e.g., mutual information between state and next character, under a Markov assumption)?

2. **Algebraic characterization of the softmax boundary.** The query-indexed monoid decomposition (Proposition above) suggests that softmax attention lives in a category of "parameterized monoid homomorphisms." Is there a universal object in this category, analogous to T(V) for plain monoid homomorphisms?

3. **Structured higher-order tensors.** T₃(R^d) captures trigram correlations but has dimension `1 + d + d^2 + d^3`. Do symmetric tensors, exterior algebras, or other structured algebras provide a more parameter-efficient alternative while preserving the monoid property?

4. **The feature map gap.** The gap between linear attention (monoid) and softmax attention (non-monoid) is measured by how well a finite-dimensional feature map approximates the softmax kernel. Can this gap be characterized algebraically (e.g., as the error of a finite-rank approximation to a specific kernel)?

5. **Position from algebra.** Our monoid models are position-blind (the state is a commutative sum). Positional information could enter through position-dependent embeddings (making the map `Char -> V` also depend on position), which would break the monoid in a controlled way. Is there an algebraic framework for "monoid + position" that preserves some compositionality?
